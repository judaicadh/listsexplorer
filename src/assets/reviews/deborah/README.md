# Die Deborah: full-folder screening and transcription record

100 supplied PDFs, 1,423 PDF scans. All files had searchable text extracted and all pages received first-pass layout screening. Candidate named enumerations were checked in context. This is a list-extraction project, not a transcription of every article, story, advertisement or page.

64 news/institutional lists, 715 entries, transcribed and compared to scans (including the first published eight lists / 59 entries). These include officers, committees, supporters, beneficiaries, bequests, prizes and named participants. No identities inferred. Amounts are assigned only when printed; gifts, institutional allocations, prize money and operating costs are distinguished. Reports of the same event are grouped into sections rather than split geographically.

One combined Jewish historical anniversaries calendar: 2362 working rows across the twelve 1901 monthly installments and supplied 1902 supplements. These rows are an OCR draft, NOT a fully proofread transcription. Every calendar event is marked uncertain. Month boundaries and source-page references were checked; names, descriptions and historical dates still require row-by-row proofreading. Calendar names are not linked to personal identities. Newspaper corrections are represented explicitly, not silently substituted.

Coverage limitations: first-pass screening is not line-by-line proofreading of all 1,423 scans. Short enumerations embedded in prose may remain undetected. Incidental people in narrative, fiction, advertisements and aggregate operating statistics were not automatically turned into member/donor lists. “No structured named list selected” records the result of this pass, not proof that a page has no possible list.

Files: issue-inventory.json (all 100 filenames), page-inventory.json (all 1,423 scans), list-checks.json (list/page review status), progress.json (counts). calendar-full-ocr.txt retains unproofread source-page OCR; calendar-unparsed.json records non-event lines left outside event rows. calendar-record.json retains page-level provenance for the working extraction.

Bibliographic corrections: first filename says volume 29 no. 2 but masthead is no. 27, 1 January 1886. Volume 31 no. 30 is 20 January 1888, despite filename year 1887. Volume 40 no. 1 cover says 1 July 1894; inside masthead says 5 July 1894, used as publication date. The 1901–1902 new-series issues are monthly. Supplied dates have gaps.

Source credit: user-supplied digitized PDFs; metadata credits Internet Archive. No original online item identifier has been established. Transcriptions are AI-assisted and have not had independent human proofreading.
